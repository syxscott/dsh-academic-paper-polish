# Conodont diversity of the Middle Ordovician Honghuayu section, Yangtze Basin

## Abstract

We describe a new assemblage of coniform elements recovered from the Middle Ordovician Honghuayu section of the Yangtze Basin. The material was studied with SEM and ICP-MS, and the diagnostic elements are shown in Figure 3. The assemblage is the first to document the full diversity of the genus in this area, filling a gap in the regional record. The diversity is very high, and the topic has attracted widespread attention (Smith et al., 2020). These data indicate a warm, shallow shelf setting for the interval.

## Introduction

Conodont elements are among the most abundant fossils in the Ordovician of the Yangtze Basin and are widely used for biostratigraphy. The taxonomy of the genus Panderodus remains unclear, and the deficiency of the previous approach is well known. The aims of this paper are to describe the assemblage and to assess its stratigraphic value. We collected material from two sections in the Yangtze Basin during the 2021 field season. Previous work has not been examined in detail for the Honghuayu section, and the regional fauna still remains poorly understood. For the first time we show the complete conodont assemblage of the Honghuayu section and compare it with the neighbouring Linxiang section.

## Methods

Twelve samples were collected from the Honghuayu section between the 34 m and 41 m levels. The samples were processed in dilute hydrochloric acid, and the residues were sieved and dried. All coniform elements were picked under a stereo microscope, and 320 elements were measured at ×40 magnification. The measurements were processed in Python 3.11, and outliers were excluded from the dataset. The specimen registration numbers are listed in the appendix.

## Results

The assemblage contains 24 species and 180 individuals, and the elements range from 120 µm to 480 µm in length. These results indicate that the diversity increased up-section, and the elements of Panderodus dominate the assemblage. Surprisingly, the platform elements are rare and poorly preserved. The measured values are summarised in the appendix, and the full dataset is available from the repository.

## Discussion

The diversity pattern is consistent with a warm shelf environment and with the regional literature. The elements are preserved as isolated jaws and the morphology is quite variable between samples. We therefore propose that the genus was widespread in the Yangtze Basin during the Middle Ordovician. Some more samples are needed to test this hypothesis, and the taxonomy of the genus still requires revision. The paleogeographic implications of the finding are important for regional correlation.

## Conclusion

We found that conodont diversity in the Yangtze Basin was high during the Middle Ordovician. The genus is very widely known from the literature (Smith et al., 2020), and it has been widely studied in the region. The paleogeographic implications of the finding are important for regional correlation.
